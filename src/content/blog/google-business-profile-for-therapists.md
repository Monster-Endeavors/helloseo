A Google Business Profile for therapists is the listing that shows your practice on Google Maps and in the local results above the regular search results. When someone searches "therapist near me", these listings are often the first thing they see, before any website. A complete profile is one of the fastest ways for a private practice to get found locally.

This checklist covers the setup we do for practices that use our [done-for-you SEO service](/done-for-you-seo-aeo).

## Who can create a Google Business Profile?

Any licensed therapist who meets clients in person at a fixed address can create one. If you only see clients online, you can still list your practice, but you must hide your address and set a service area instead. Group practices should create one profile for the practice, and each clinician can have their own profile if they are listed publicly at that address.

## The setup checklist

Work through these fields in order. Each one gives Google a clearer picture of who you help and where:

1. **Business name.** Use your real practice name. Adding keywords such as "anxiety therapy" to the name breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
2. **Primary category.** Pick the closest match, usually "Psychotherapist", "Counselor" or "Marriage counselor".
3. **Secondary categories.** Add the other services you actually offer.
4. **Services.** List each service with a short, plain-language description.
5. **Hours and booking link.** Link straight to your booking or consultation page.
6. **Description.** Write it for clients: who you help, how sessions work, and which insurance you take.
7. **Photos.** Add your office, the entrance and a professional headshot.

## Which categories should a therapist choose?

Your primary category matters most, so choose the one that matches your main service. Use secondary categories for the rest:

| If you mainly offer | Primary category | Useful secondary categories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual therapy | Psychotherapist | Counselor, Mental health service |
| Couples counseling | Marriage counselor | Family counselor, Psychotherapist |
| Child and teen therapy | Child psychologist | Family counselor, Counselor |

## How should therapists ask for reviews?

Reviews help your profile rank, but many licensing boards limit how therapists can ask current clients for testimonials. Check your board's rules before you ask anyone. Where asking is not allowed, focus on reviews from colleagues and referral partners on directories, and reply to every review without confirming that the reviewer is a client.

## Keep the profile active

Google favors profiles that stay current. Post an update once a month, add new photos each season, and answer questions in the Q&A section. Make sure your name, address and phone number match your website and every directory exactly, because mismatches weaken the signals Google uses to trust a local listing.
